The last mile of ownership

Every session so far changed how you USE the model: prompts, reasoning modes, tools, serving. None of it changed the model. Fine-tuning does - and because Hermes itself is a fine-tune (a base model plus the Nous SFT recipe), adding your own layer of supervised fine-tuning on top is not a hack, it is the same move Nous made, one step further down the chain. But the craft here starts before any GPU spins up: the single most important skill in fine-tuning is knowing when NOT to do it. That decision gate is Part 1.

Part 1 · the decision gate

Should you even fine-tune? ~10 min read

Fine-tuning is the most permanent, most expensive way to change model behavior - and most behavior changes do not need it. Climb the ladder from the bottom: each rung is cheaper, faster, and easier to undo than the one above it.

Self-studyThe escalation ladder - climb before you train4 min read▶

Almost every "we need to fine-tune" request dissolves on a lower rung. Work the ladder in order:

  • Rung 1 - system prompt. Persona, tone, rules, output format: Session 3 showed that Hermes treats the system prompt as its constitution. If the behavior can be described in words, describe it. Free, instant, reversible.
  • Rung 2 - few-shot examples. Hard-to-describe formats are easy to demonstrate. Paste 3-5 gold-standard input/output pairs into the prompt and the model imitates them. Still zero training.
  • Rung 3 - RAG. If the gap is KNOWLEDGE (your docs, your product catalog, this week's prices), fine-tuning is the wrong tool anyway - retrieval fetches current facts at ask time, and updating means updating a folder, not retraining.
  • Rung 4 - fine-tune. Only when the behavior must live in the weights: too consistent, too pervasive, or too latency-critical for prompting to carry it.
★ Try it now - the gate, as a promptI am considering fine-tuning a local 14B model. The behavior I want: [describe it]. Argue against me: which of these would get 90% of the result - a better system prompt, few-shot examples in the prompt, or retrieval over my documents? Only if all three genuinely fail, tell me what my fine-tuning dataset would need to contain.
Real world

The support-bot rescue. A team spent two weekends preparing a fine-tuning dataset to make their assistant "answer in our support style". A reviewer pasted their style guide into the system prompt plus four example answers - rung 1 and 2 - and matched the fine-tune's quality before the first training run finished. The dataset work was not wasted: it became their eval set.

Self-studyThe four good reasons to fine-tune3 min read▶

When the ladder genuinely runs out, these are the four jobs where fine-tuning earns its cost:

ReasonWhy prompting cannot carry it
Consistent format or voice at scaleA prompt holds a format for one conversation; a fine-tune holds it across a million calls, with no drift and no 800-token instruction block on every request
Domain vocabularyYour industry's jargon, codes, and phrasing patterns - things the base model has barely seen - become native instead of explained-per-prompt
LatencyBaking instructions into the weights deletes the long system prompt: fewer input tokens per call, faster time to first token, cheaper serving at volume
Offline distillationCapture a bigger model's behavior on YOUR task into a small local model: generate training pairs with the big one, fine-tune the small one, run it offline forever

Notice what is NOT on the list: adding knowledge. Facts belong in RAG, where they stay current and citable. Fine-tuning teaches the model how to BEHAVE, not what is true this week.

Self-studyThe honest risks - forgetting, and the eval discipline3 min read▶

Fine-tuning has one classic failure mode, and one discipline that catches it:

  • Catastrophic forgetting. A narrow fine-tune drags the weights toward your data - and away from everything else. Train hard on invoice extraction and the model may get measurably worse at reasoning, coding, or holding a normal conversation. The narrower the dataset and the harder you train, the more general skill you burn.
  • The eval-before-and-after discipline. Before training, freeze a small held-out eval set: ~10 prompts covering your target task AND a few general-skill checks (a reasoning question, a summarization, a normal chat). Run them against the stock model and save the answers. After training, run the identical set. Your task prompts should get better; the general ones must not get meaningfully worse. No before-numbers means no way to know what you broke.
The two cheapest insurance policies Keep training gentle (LoRA at modest rank, few epochs, early stop) and keep the stock model installed next to your fine-tune in Ollama. If the custom model degrades on general work, routing around it is one model-name swap - because you never overwrote anything.
Part 2 · covers the Unsloth Hermes docs + the Hermes 3 dataset

The LoRA path ~12 min read

Full fine-tuning of a 14B model means updating 14 billion numbers - a multi-GPU, many-dollars job. LoRA is the trick that collapses it onto a single consumer GPU: freeze the model, train a small set of adapter matrices beside it.

Self-studyWhat LoRA and QLoRA actually do4 min read▶

LoRA (Low-Rank Adaptation) starts from an empirical observation: the change a fine-tune makes to a weight matrix is highly redundant - it can be approximated by two thin matrices whose product is the update. So instead of touching the 14B frozen parameters, you attach small low-rank adapter matrices to key layers and train ONLY those - typically a few percent of the model's parameters, often less.

  • Memory collapses. No optimizer state for 14B parameters, no gradients through frozen weights stored for update - the training footprint shrinks from multi-GPU to single-GPU.
  • QLoRA goes further: load the frozen base in 4-bit quantization (Session 2 taught you quants) and train the adapters in higher precision on top. That is the trick that fits a 14B fine-tune onto a free Colab T4.
  • The output is an adapter file, megabytes in size. Keep several adapters for one base, swap them per task, or merge one into the base for deployment (Part 3).

The intuition to keep: LoRA does not rewrite the model's knowledge, it learns a low-rank NUDGE on top of it. That is also why gentle LoRA runs are your first defense against catastrophic forgetting - the nudge is structurally small.

Self-studyUnsloth - the 14B on a free Colab4 min read▶

Unsloth is the open-source fine-tuning framework that has become the community default for exactly our situation: QLoRA on consumer hardware, with heavy kernel-level optimization for speed and memory. Two things make it the right on-ramp here:

  • Official Hermes integration. Unsloth's documentation (unsloth.ai/docs) carries Hermes-specific guidance - you are not adapting a generic tutorial, you are following a supported path for the model family this course runs on.
  • Notebook-first workflow. The canonical Unsloth experience is a Colab notebook: load the model 4-bit, attach LoRA adapters, point at your dataset, train, export. A run on a few hundred examples finishes in well under an hour on the free tier.

One warning, made with love: the Unsloth ecosystem moves FAST - APIs, notebook links, and recommended settings change month to month. This page teaches you the shape of the recipe; for the exact cells, always open the current official notebook from the Unsloth docs rather than a copy from a six-month-old blog post.

Hardware reality check Training needs an NVIDIA GPU (Colab's free tier counts). Your Mac's strength is INFERENCE - so the loop is: train in the cloud for an hour, then bring the result home to Ollama, where it runs offline forever. Part 3 is that homecoming.
Self-studyDataset shape - ChatML in, and the reference library4 min read▶

Hermes models are SFT products: they were made by supervised fine-tuning on conversations. Your dataset speaks the same language - a list of conversations in the ChatML roles you have used since Session 3:

★ One training example (conceptual shape){"conversations": [ {"role": "system", "content": "You are the claims triage assistant for an insurance team..."}, {"role": "user", "content": "Customer reports water damage, policy HX-2291, second claim this year..."}, {"role": "assistant", "content": "SEVERITY: medium\nPOLICY CHECK: active, water damage covered with $500 excess\nROUTE TO: property claims queue\n..."} ]}
  • Quality beats quantity, always. 100-500 excellent examples of exactly the behavior you want outperform 10,000 scraped mediocre ones. Every assistant turn is a demonstration the model will imitate - including its mistakes.
  • Cover the variation you expect. Easy cases, hard cases, edge cases, and a few "this is out of scope, escalate" examples so the tuned model keeps its judgment.
  • Your reference for "what good looks like": the Hermes 3 training dataset, which Nous released publicly on HuggingFace in July 2025. It is the actual corpus a production Hermes was trained on - browse it and you are reading the house style: how system prompts are written, how assistant turns demonstrate format-following, how tool calls and reasoning are represented. Before writing your own 300 examples, read 30 of theirs.
Real world

The dataset that was the product. A two-person team built a contract-clause tagger. Their fine-tune took 40 minutes; their dataset took three weeks - a lawyer reviewing every assistant turn. Two model generations later, the dataset had outlived both base models: they re-tuned the newest Hermes on the same 400 examples in an afternoon. The durable asset was never the weights.

Part 3 · covers the Ollama Modelfile docs

Ship it back to Ollama ~8 min read

A trained adapter in a Colab is not yet YOUR model. Three moves bring it home: merge the adapter into the base, convert to GGUF, and wrap it in a Modelfile so it joins your Session 2 lineup by name.

Self-studyMerge, then GGUF - from adapter to artifact3 min read▶

Two conversions stand between "training finished" and "runs on my Mac":

  • Merge. Fold the LoRA adapter's nudge into the base weights, producing one standalone model that behaves like base-plus-adapter with nothing to attach at runtime. Unsloth does this with a single export call at the end of the notebook.
  • Convert to GGUF. GGUF is the quantized single-file format your local stack has eaten since Session 2 - the same format as every model you pulled with ollama run. Unsloth's export can write GGUF directly (pick a quant like Q4_K_M, exactly the sizing logic from Session 2), or you convert the merged model with llama.cpp's conversion script.

The result is one .gguf file - your fine-tune, in the same shape as every other local model you own. Download it from Colab and it is yours, offline, forever.

Self-studyThe Modelfile - wrap it and name it3 min read▶

Session 2's Modelfile skill pays off here. Point FROM at your local GGUF instead of a registry name, bake in the official sampling numbers, and set the system prompt your fine-tune expects:

★ Modelfile for your fine-tuneFROM ./my-hermes.gguf PARAMETER temperature 0.6 PARAMETER top_p 0.95 PARAMETER top_k 20 PARAMETER num_ctx 16384 SYSTEM You are the claims triage assistant for an insurance team. Follow the triage output format exactly.

Then, from the folder containing both files:

★ Create and run itollama create my-hermes -f Modelfile ollama run my-hermes

Run ollama list and enjoy the moment: my-hermes sits next to hermes3:8b and your Session 2 pulls - a model whose behavior you shaped, addressable by name from everything you built in Sessions 5-8 (JSON pipelines, tool loops, even Hermes Agent can point at it).

Match the template Your fine-tune inherits the base model's chat template - for the 14B that is ChatML. If your model gives strange, format-broken answers, template mismatch in the Modelfile is the first thing to check; copy the TEMPLATE block from the base model's Modelfile (ollama show --modelfile) if needed.
Self-studyLicense carry-through - what your fine-tune inherits2 min read▶

Session 7's license lesson has one extra clause for fine-tuners: your fine-tune inherits the base model's license. Training on top does not launder the terms.

  • Llama-based Hermes (Hermes 3, Hermes 4 70B/405B): a fine-tune is a derivative under the Llama 3 license - its naming, distribution, and use conditions carry through to YOUR model.
  • Apache 2.0 Hermes (the Qwen-based Hermes 4 14B, Hermes 4.3-36B): the clean case - permissive terms, straightforward commercial use, no naming obligations. One more reason the 14B is this course's hero.
  • Shipping to customers? Read the actual license of the actual base before you distribute weights, and note the dataset side too: data you train on must be data you have the right to use.
The recipe

End to end - dataset to ollama run ★ one afternoon

The whole path, in order. Steps 1 and 4 are where your time should go; the middle is increasingly push-button.

Prepare 100-500 ChatML examples. Real inputs from your task, gold-standard assistant answers, consistent system prompt. Browse 30 conversations from the public Hermes 3 dataset first to calibrate what good looks like. Set aside ~10 extra prompts as your held-out eval set - they must never enter training.

Baseline eval. Run the 10 held-out prompts (plus 2-3 general-skill checks) against stock Hermes 4 14B and save every answer. This is your "before".

Open the current official Unsloth notebook for Hermes/Qwen3-family QLoRA from unsloth.ai/docs - follow the live version, it moves fast. Load the 14B in 4-bit, attach LoRA adapters, point at your dataset.

Train gently. Modest rank, 1-3 epochs, watch the loss curve. On a free Colab GPU a few hundred examples train in tens of minutes. Resist the urge to train harder "to be sure" - that is how forgetting happens.

Eval after. Same 10 + general prompts against the tuned model. Task answers better, general answers intact? Proceed. General skill visibly degraded? Lower the epochs or rank and rerun - this is the checkpoint that protects you.

Export GGUF. Merge the adapter and export to GGUF (Q4_K_M is the sane default) from the notebook. Download the file.

Modelfile + create. Write the Modelfile from Part 3 (FROM ./my-hermes.gguf, the official sampling PARAMETERs, your SYSTEM line), then ollama create my-hermes -f Modelfile.

Run it. ollama run my-hermes, wifi off if you like. A model you reshaped, running on hardware you own, answering in the behavior you taught it. That is the last mile, walked.

Keep both models installed Your fine-tune is a specialist; stock Hermes stays the generalist. Route your task to my-hermes and everything else to the stock model - Ollama serves both by name, and Session 6's tool loop can pick per request.
Homework

Try it yourself - this week ◐ 2-4 hours total

  • Run the decision gate honestly: write down the behavior you want, then attempt it with a system prompt (rung 1) and few-shot examples (rung 2). Only proceed if both genuinely fall short - and keep the failed attempts as evidence.
  • Read 30 conversations from the public Hermes 3 dataset on HuggingFace. Note three things their assistant turns do that yours should copy.
  • Build a starter dataset: 50 ChatML examples for your task, plus a 10-prompt held-out eval set. (50 is enough for a first experiment; grow to 100-500 for the real run.)
  • Run the recipe end to end on the free Colab tier, including the before/after eval - and write one sentence on what changed, good and bad.
  • Ship it home: export GGUF, write the Modelfile, ollama create, and show a colleague ollama list with your model's name in it.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Your assistant needs to answer from this week's product catalog. Fine-tune?

The escalation ladder: facts that change belong in retrieval, where updating means updating a folder. Fine-tune for consistent format/voice, domain vocabulary, latency, or distillation - not for this week's prices.

2 · What does LoRA train?

Frozen base plus tiny trainable adapters is the whole trick - it collapses the memory footprint onto one consumer GPU (QLoRA adds a 4-bit base, fitting a free Colab), and the structurally small nudge is your first defense against forgetting.

3 · What must happen BEFORE your first training run?

Eval before and after is the discipline of the whole page: task prompts should improve, general-skill prompts must not degrade. Without before-numbers you cannot know what your fine-tune broke.

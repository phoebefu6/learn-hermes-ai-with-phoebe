Part 0

The gear you did not know it had

Session 3 gave your assistant a constitution. Today we give it judgment about effort. Hermes 4 is a hybrid reasoning model: by default it answers instantly, but on demand it will deliberate - visibly, inside think tags - before answering. Used on the right problems, that one switch turns near-random performance into near-perfect. Used on the wrong problems, it just makes your laptop hot. Learning the difference is the whole session.

Part 1 · covers the Hermes 4 report, section 3

One model, two gears 6 min live

Some labs ship two separate models - a fast one and a "reasoning" one - and make you choose at download time. Hermes 4 puts both behaviors in one set of weights. The default gear answers like every chat model you know. The reasoning gear makes the model deliberate with itself first, inside <think> </think> tags, and only then produce the final answer.

LiveThink tags - deliberation you can read3 min▶

When the reasoning gear is on, the model writes its internal monologue between <think> and </think> before the visible answer. Inside the tags you will watch it restate the problem, try an approach, catch its own mistake, and start over - like a scratchpad you get to read. Three things to internalize:

  • It is the same model. No second download, no different weights. The gear is selected by how you prompt it, not by what you installed.
  • The model budgets its own deliberation. Easy question in reasoning mode: a few lines of thought. Genuinely hard one: pages. You do not set a thinking length; the model decides when it is done.
  • The scratchpad is not the answer. The text inside the tags is exploratory - it can wander, backtrack, and be wrong on the way to being right. Only what comes after </think> is the model's actual response.
Chat apps hide this from you Hosted reasoning models show a "thinking..." spinner and hide the monologue. Running Hermes locally, you see every word of the deliberation. That transparency is one of the best learning tools in this course - and Demo 1 is built around reading it together.
LiveThe 30k self-stop - a great engineering yarn3 min▶

Free-running deliberation has a failure mode: the model thinks so long it runs out of room. Every model has a context limit, and a chain of thought that never closes its </think> tag means no answer at all.

Real world

The model that talked itself off a cliff. During Hermes 4 evaluation, the 14B model was hitting its 40,960-token context limit on 60% of LiveCodeBench reasoning runs - it thought right up to the wall and never got to answer. The Nous fix was elegant: a second SFT stage that teaches the model to wrap up and close </think> by roughly 30k tokens. The cost was honest and small - a few percent on the hardest benchmarks (in that experiment's frame, the 405B went from 55.0 to 52.4 on AIME'24). The trade: slightly shorter maximum thinking in exchange for always actually finishing. An unfinished genius answer scores zero.

Remember this story when you size context windows: your hermes4-14b-16k build from Session 2 has 16,384 tokens of room, and a deep reasoning run can genuinely fill it. If reasoning answers get cut off, context length is the first suspect.

Self-studyWhy hybrid beats a dedicated reasoning model2 min read▶

Dedicated reasoning models deliberate on everything - including "write a two-line email", where deliberation adds nothing but latency. Dedicated instant models can never slow down for the problem that deserves it. A hybrid gives you a per-question choice with zero switching cost: same weights, same memory footprint, same Ollama tag, just a different prompt. On a laptop, where RAM is the scarce resource and every loaded model competes for it, one model that does both is not a compromise - it is the win. The Hermes 4 technical report (section 3) covers how both behaviors were trained into a single checkpoint.

When thinking pays 6 min live

Reasoning mode is not "smart mode". It is a targeted tool with a measurable profile: transformative on multi-step problems, near-useless on everything else. Nous published the numbers, so we do not have to guess.

LiveThe evidence - same model, two gears, hard math3 min▶

These are Hermes 4 405B scores with reasoning on versus off, from the technical report's evaluations. Read the AIME rows twice:

Task typeInstantReasoningVerdict

The pattern: when a problem has steps that build on each other - and a wrong early step poisons everything after it - deliberation is worth 5-7x. When a problem is retrieval, phrasing, or taste, the instant gear was already fine.

LiveThe rule of thumb - and what a laptop actually pays3 min▶

Your ON/OFF instinct, in one table:

Reasoning ONReasoning OFF
Multi-step logic and constraint puzzlesFactual lookups and definitions
Math with intermediate resultsDrafts, emails, rewrites, summaries

Tokens are seconds on your machine On a laptop pushing maybe 10-25 tokens per second, a 3,000-token deliberation is two to five minutes of fan noise before the answer even starts - and it eats your 16k context window too. Hosted models hide this cost behind someone else's GPUs; locally you feel every token. That is not a bug: it builds exactly the budgeting instinct this session is about.

The three switches 5 min live

Three ways to flip the gear, from most universal to most programmatic. All three end in the same place: the model deliberating inside think tags.

LiveSwitch 1 · the system prompt (works everywhere)2 min▶

The original switch, introduced with DeepHermes-3 and carried into Hermes 4: a system prompt that instructs the model to deliberate in think tags. This is the exact published prompt - use it verbatim:

★ The reasoning toggle prompt (verbatim, from the DeepHermes-3 model card)You are a deep thinking AI, you may use extremely long chains of thought to deeply consider the problem and deliberate with yourself via systematic reasoning processes to help come to a correct solution prior to answering. You should enclose your thoughts and internal monologue inside <think> </think> tags, and then provide your solution or response to the problem.

Because it is just a system prompt, it works in Ollama, LM Studio, Python, or any chat UI - and it stacks with what you learned in Session 3: put it in a Modelfile SYSTEM block and you have a permanent thinking variant of your assistant (that is tonight's homework).

LiveSwitch 2 · the LM Studio toggle (one click)1 min▶

LM Studio ships native support for Hermes 4's hybrid mode: load a Hermes 4 model and you get a UI toggle that turns reasoning on and off per chat - no prompt engineering required. There is a second option next to it worth knowing: keep the chain of thought in subsequent turns, so earlier deliberations stay in the conversation the model sees. On by default, prior thinking is dropped each turn to save context; keep it when later turns need to build on the reasoning itself, not just the conclusions.

LiveSwitch 3 · the chat-template flag (for code)2 min▶

When you drive Hermes from Python with the HuggingFace tokenizer, the chat template exposes reasoning as a flag - no prompt text needed:

★ Python - reasoning via the chat templateprompt = tokenizer.apply_chat_template( messages, thinking=True, # flip the reasoning gear on keep_cots=True, # retain prior chains of thought across turns add_generation_prompt=True, tokenize=False, )

thinking=True is the gear; keep_cots=True is the multi-turn memory of deliberations - the programmatic twin of LM Studio's keep-CoT checkbox. Session 5 onward we mostly drive Hermes from Python, so this switch becomes your daily one.

The two-gear experiment ★ 12 min · everyone follows

One genuinely hard puzzle, both gears, same model on your machine. The goal is to watch the difference, not be told about it - and to read a real chain of thought together.

★ The puzzle (paste it in both gears)Ana, Ben, and Cara each need one 1-hour meeting slot from: 9am, 10am, 11am, 1pm. Constraints: (1) Ana must meet before 11am. (2) Ben's slot must be immediately after Cara's. (3) Cara will not take the 9am slot. Who gets which slot, and which slot stays free? Explain your final assignment.

Instant gear first: ollama run hermes4-14b-16k, paste the puzzle, note the answer and roughly how fast it arrived. Small models in instant mode often stumble on constraint chaining - if it gets it right, ask "are you sure? check each constraint" and watch how confident it is either way.

Flip the gear without leaving Ollama: type /set system "..." with the full DeepHermes toggle prompt from Part 3 between the quotes (have it in your clipboard before class).

Paste the same puzzle again. Watch the <think> block stream: it will enumerate the slots, test Cara-Ben pairings, hit the 11am constraint, backtrack, and settle. Read it aloud together - this is the model showing its work.

Check the answer against the constraints as a group (it should be: Ana 9am, Cara 10am, Ben 11am, 1pm free). Then compare: instant gear's confidence versus reasoning gear's visible verification.

Count the cost too: the reasoning run took noticeably longer and produced many times the tokens. Both observations - better answer, real cost - are the lesson.

If the instant gear happens to get it right Small puzzles sometimes fall to a lucky pattern-match. Do not fight it - the reasoning run is still the demo: the visible verification inside the think tags is what instant mode cannot give you, even when its answer is correct.

The LM Studio route - and where thinking does NOT pay ★ 8 min · one click

Same model, second switch: LM Studio's UI toggle. Then the counter-demo everyone needs - an everyday question in both gears, to watch reasoning cost real time for nothing.

Open LM Studio (optional install from Session 2) and load your Hermes 4 14B. In the chat panel, find the reasoning toggle - note the keep-CoT option beside it.

Toggle reasoning ON and paste the Demo 1 puzzle. Same think-tags behavior as the system-prompt route - two switches, one gear. LM Studio folds the thinking into a collapsible block; expand it.

Now the counter-demo. Toggle reasoning OFF and ask the everyday question below. Instant, perfectly good answer.

Toggle reasoning ON and ask the exact same question. Watch it deliberate about... a thank-you note. The answer is not better - you just paid seconds and tokens for nothing.

Say the rule out loud one more time: multi-step logic, math, tricky code, careful analysis = ON. Lookups, drafts, chat = OFF.

Real world

The analyst who left thinking on. An analyst set the reasoning prompt as their assistant's permanent default after it nailed a tricky reconciliation. A week later every "rephrase this Slack message" took ninety seconds and the assistant felt broken. The fix was this session's design: two named variants - the everyday one and a hermes4-think - and the judgment to pick per task.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What are the <think> </think> tags?

Same weights, second gear: in reasoning mode the model deliberates inside the tags - exploring, backtracking, self-correcting - then closes them and gives the real answer.

2 · When does reasoning mode actually pay?

The published deltas are the rule of thumb: hard math and logic transform (11.4 → 81.9 on AIME'24); lookups, drafts, and chat just cost you tokens and seconds.

3 · Which setting keeps prior chains of thought in the conversation across turns?

thinking=True flips the gear for a turn; keep_cots=True (and LM Studio's matching checkbox) is what retains earlier deliberations so later turns can build on them.

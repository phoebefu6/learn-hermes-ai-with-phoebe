What Hermes actually is 7 min live

Hermes is not one model - it is a family of open-weights models, each built by taking a strong base model and post-training it with Nous Research's data recipe. Get the family map right and every later session gets easier.

LiveOpen weights - what you actually get3 min▶

When Nous releases a Hermes model, they publish the weights - the full set of learned numbers that IS the model - on HuggingFace, for anyone to download. That single fact changes your relationship with AI:

  • You can run it yourself. On your laptop, on a server, on a machine with no internet. Your prompts never leave the room.
  • Nobody can take it away. A subscription model can be deprecated, price-hiked, or policy-changed overnight. A model on your disk is yours forever.
  • You can modify it. Quantize it smaller, fine-tune it on your data, embed it in a product (license permitting - Session 7 covers licenses).

The tradeoff is honest: hosted frontier models are bigger and come with a support team. Open weights trade a little peak capability for total ownership. This course teaches you to get the most out of that trade.

Real world

The lawyer's laptop. A solo lawyer wants AI help on client files but cannot send privileged documents to any cloud. A local Hermes on a MacBook reads every file offline - the documents never leave the machine. That use case is impossible with any subscription chatbot, at any price.

Self-studySame body, different values - how a Hermes is made2 min read▶

Hermes 4 70B and Llama 3.1 70B have the same architecture and started from the same weights. So what did Nous change? The post-training: roughly 5 million carefully synthesized examples (about 19 billion tokens for Hermes 4, 60 billion for 4.3) that teach the base model how to behave - how to follow a system prompt faithfully, call tools, produce valid JSON, reason step by step, and skip the moralizing.

Deep-dive D1 walks through the actual machinery (DataForge graphs, rejection sampling) for the curious - it is genuinely elegant engineering, and all of it is published in the Hermes 4 technical report.

Self-studyWho is Nous Research?2 min read▶

Nous Research started as a loose collective of open-source finetuners and grew into one of the most influential open-model labs. Their naming is deliberately hermetic - Nous is Greek for divine intellect, and the stack reads like a mythology shelf: Hermes (the messenger), Psyche (the distributed training network), Atropos (the RL environments framework), Nomos (a math model).

  • Track record: Hermes 2 Pro set the open standard for function calling; Hermes 3 was the first full finetune of Llama 3.1 405B; the tool-calling format Hermes pioneered is now literally a named parser in vLLM (--tool-call-parser hermes).
  • Radical openness: weights, datasets (the Hermes 3 dataset is public), technical reports, and even the training experiments are published.
  • The 2026 headline: Hermes Agent - their open-source autonomous agent - became one of the fastest-growing open projects ever, and the company was last reported in funding talks at a $1.5B valuation.

Neutral alignment - aligned to you 6 min live

Every big-lab model ships with a policy layer: topics it deflects, tones it refuses, opinions it sands down - decided by the lab, not by you. The Hermes bet is different: make the model follow the user's system prompt as faithfully as possible, and let the human own the values. Nous calls it neutral alignment, and it is the single most important idea in this course.

LiveWhat "aligned to you" means in practice3 min▶

Ask a heavily-aligned model to play a ruthless negotiation counterpart for practice, and you often get a lecture about kindness instead. Hermes takes the system prompt as its constitution: tell it who to be, and it commits. Three practical consequences:

  • The system prompt is a steering wheel, not a suggestion. Personas hold. Rules hold. Formats hold. Session 3 is entirely about exploiting this.
  • Far fewer refusals on legitimate work. Edgy marketing copy, adversarial role-play, security education, dark fiction - Hermes engages where other models deflect.
  • No hidden thumb on the scale. Ask for both sides of a contested question and you get both sides, argued properly, without a lab's house opinion leaking in.

Nous measures this with RefusalBench, their published benchmark of borderline-but-legitimate prompts. Hermes 4 405B engages with 57% of them; the big hosted models manage roughly 17%. Hermes 4.3 pushes to 75%. Whatever you think the right number is, the gap is the product.

Real world

The sales trainer. A sales team wanted to rehearse against realistic difficult buyers. Their hosted AI kept breaking character to be supportive. The same role-play prompt on Hermes stays in character for a full session - which is exactly what makes the practice useful.

LiveThe other side of the deal: you own safety2 min▶

Neutral alignment is a transfer of responsibility, not an absence of it. When the lab stops deciding what is appropriate, that decision lands on whoever writes the system prompt - you. Treat these as house rules from day one:

  • Steerable is not omniscient. Hermes hallucinates like every LLM. Low refusal means it will also confidently attempt things it should decline for accuracy reasons - your verification habits matter MORE here, not less.
  • Your system prompt is your policy. Deploying Hermes for a team or a product? Write the guardrails into the system prompt explicitly - tone limits, forbidden topics, escalation rules. Session 3 gives you the pattern.
  • The law still applies to you. An open model on your laptop does not change what is legal or ethical to produce and ship. The model follows you; accountability follows you too.
The house rule for this course We use Hermes's openness for legitimate work: personas, candor, edge-case testing, private data. "It will do what I say" is the tool; "I stand behind what I ship" is the license for it.

Demo 1 of 2

First contact - no install needed ★ 8 min · everyone follows

Before we install anything, meet the model where it is easiest: a hosted Hermes in the browser. The goal is to FEEL the neutral-alignment difference, not just hear about it.

Open chat.nousresearch.com (Nous Chat, free account) - or, alternate route, openrouter.ai where Hermes 3 405B has a free tier in the model playground.

Warm-up: ask something you would ask any chatbot. Confirm it is just a very good LLM - fluent, fast, ordinary. The differences are in the steering, not the small talk.

Now run the "Red" negotiation prompt from Part 2. Push it: be difficult, go three rounds, try to make it break character. Notice it does not.

Steering test: "Answer the next question twice: once as a cautious risk manager, once as an aggressive growth founder. Same facts, both fully committed." Ask a real question from your work.

Compare mentally with your daily hosted AI: where the answers feel the same, and where the persona commitment feels different. That gap is what you are here to learn to use.

Data tip Hosted Hermes is still hosted - treat Nous Chat and OpenRouter like any cloud service and keep confidential data out. The fully-private version of this conversation is exactly what Demo 2 and Session 2 set up.
Demo 2 of 2

Your first local Hermes ★ 12 min · build your own

One installer, one command, and a Hermes is running on your machine with no internet required. We use the friendliest on-ramp today (Hermes 3 8B via Ollama); Session 2 upgrades you to the full lineup.

Install Ollama: download from ollama.com (Mac / Windows / Linux) and run the installer. It lives in your menu bar and serves models locally.

Open a terminal and run the one-liner below. First run downloads ~4.7GB, then drops you into a chat prompt.

Say hello. Then prove it is local: turn OFF your wifi and ask another question. It answers. Let that land - a capable AI is running entirely on your hardware.

Re-run the "Red" negotiation prompt from Demo 1, now fully offline. Same steerability, zero cloud.

Type /bye to exit. Run ollama list to see the model sitting on your disk - that file is yours.

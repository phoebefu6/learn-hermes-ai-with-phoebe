Part 1 · covers the Hermes Agent provider docs + OpenRouter listings

The provider menu 8 min live

Three ways to reach a hosted Hermes, all OpenAI-compatible, all reachable from the exact code you already wrote. The differences are pricing, extras, and who you are trusting with your prompts.

LiveNous Portal - the first-party API3 min▶

The Portal (portal.nousresearch.com) is Nous Research's own API - money and prompts go to the people who make the model. The essentials:

  • Endpoint: https://inference-api.nousresearch.com/v1, fully OpenAI-compatible. API keys come from the Portal dashboard.
  • Free eval tier: roughly $0.10/month in credits - not much, but enough to smoke-test your scripts against the 405B before spending anything.
  • The paid extras: paid tiers bundle a Tool Gateway - hosted tools your model can call (web search, scraping, image generation, browser use, speech). That pairs directly with your Session 6 tool loop, without you hosting the tools.
The bot-gate caveat Portal pages are bot-gated, so scripts and AI assistants (including the one writing your notes) cannot fetch live pricing. Check the current tiers yourself in a normal browser before you budget - takes one minute, and the numbers move.
LiveOpenRouter - the marketplace (and the free 405B trick)3 min▶

OpenRouter is a router across many hosting providers with one key and one endpoint. Why it matters for Hermes people:

  • The :free trick. Hermes 3 405B has a :free variant on OpenRouter - a genuinely zero-cost way to run experiments against a 405B-class Hermes. Rate-limited and best-effort, but for learning it is unbeatable. We use it in Demo 1.
  • Paid prices (as of mid-2026 - verify live): Hermes 4 405B around $1.00 in / $3.00 out per 1M tokens; Hermes 4 70B around $0.13 / $0.40. Prices move constantly on marketplaces; treat these as anchors, not gospel.
  • Provider choice for free. OpenRouter routes to whichever underlying host is up and cheapest, which is resilience you did not have to build.
★ Try it now (once you have an OpenRouter key)Point your Session 5 extract.py at base_url https://openrouter.ai/api/v1 with model nousresearch/hermes-3-llama-3.1-405b:free and run it unchanged. Cost: $0.00.
Self-studyWho actually hosts the big weights2 min read▶

Marketplaces are storefronts; someone still racks the GPUs. Worth knowing two names:

  • Nebius hosts Hermes 4 405B in FP8 and is the main underlying provider you will actually hit when you call the big model through a router.
  • Lambda was the historic launch partner that first served Hermes 3 405B back in 2024 - a nice reminder that open weights mean ANY GPU cloud can serve Hermes, which is exactly why no single provider can hold your workload hostage.

That last point is the strategic one: with closed models you rent capability from a monopoly. With open weights, hosting is a competitive commodity - if a provider raises prices, the weights are public and someone else will serve them cheaper. (Deep-dive D3 covers serving them yourself with vLLM, which even ships a named --tool-call-parser hermes.)

Part 2 · covers the model licenses on the HF cards

Licenses decide products 6 min live

"Open weights" is not one license - and if you ever ship Hermes inside a product, the license of the BASE model is what you are actually shipping under. Two minutes of table-reading now saves a legal headache later.

LiveLlama 3 license vs Apache 2.0 - what you can ship4 min▶

Every Hermes inherits the license of its base model. The lineup sorts into two buckets:

ModelLicenseShipping it in a product
Hermes 4.3 36BApache 2.0Cleanest choice for products - permissive, no special conditions
Hermes 4 14BApache-2.0-family (Qwen3 base)Very permissive - our course hero is also product-friendly
Hermes 4 70B / 405BLlama 3 licenseFine for most commercial use, with Meta's conditions attached
Hermes 3 (all sizes)Llama 3 licenseSame deal - including the :free 405B you demo with

What "Meta's conditions" means in practice: the Llama 3 license is a custom commercial license, not open source in the strict sense. The famous clause: services with more than 700 million monthly active users need a separate license from Meta (congratulations if this is your problem). There are also naming and attribution requirements. For most teams it is a non-issue - but "most" is not "all", and your legal team will ask.

The one-sentence policy Building a product? Prefer the Apache bucket (4.3 36B, 4 14B) and you never have the conversation. Using Llama-based Hermes internally or via API? Relax - that is squarely normal use.
Self-studyPrivacy tiers - what data goes where2 min read▶

The license governs what you may ship; the deployment tier governs what you expose. Keep the two separate in your head:

TierWhat leaves your machineWho can see it
Local (Ollama)Nothing. Wifi-off provably nothing (Session 1's test)You
First-party API (Portal)Prompts + outputs, under Nous's terms of serviceYou + Nous infrastructure
Marketplace (OpenRouter)Prompts + outputs, under OpenRouter's terms AND the underlying host's (e.g. Nebius)You + router + whichever provider served the call

The discipline that follows: classify your data BEFORE choosing a backend, not after. Client-confidential and regulated data stays on the local tier - that was the whole point of Sessions 1-2. Public-ish and low-stakes data can ride the APIs for capability or convenience. Read the actual ToS of any provider before routing real workloads through it; retention policies differ and change.

Part 3 · the call you will actually make

Local or hosted - the decision 4 min live

This is not a religious war, it is a routing decision - and you will make it per workload, not once. Five factors decide it.

LiveThe decision table4 min▶
FactorLocal (your machine)Hosted (Portal / OpenRouter)
PrivacyAbsolute - nothing leaves the roomProvider ToS territory - classify data first
Cost at volumeElectricity; flat regardless of callsPer token - cheap until suddenly it is not
Capability ceilingYour RAM decides - 14B to 70B for most of us405B on tap, always
LatencyNo network hop; slower tokens/sec on big modelsNetwork hop; datacenter-fast generation
Ops burdenYours: updates, uptime, the sleeping laptop problemTheirs: SLA-ish uptime, zero maintenance

The pattern that falls out for almost everyone: sensitive or high-volume-and-simple → local; occasional-but-hard or team-facing → hosted. Demo 2 turns this into a worksheet against your own real workloads.

One script, three backends ★ 10 min · build your own

The payoff for everything since Session 5: your extractor runs against a local model, a free 405B, and the first-party API - and the only thing that changes is two lines.

Open your Session 5 extract.py. Find the two lines that name a backend: the OpenAI(base_url=..., api_key=...) constructor and the model= argument. Those are the only lines that will change all demo.

Backend 1 - localhost. Run it as-is against Ollama. This is your baseline: free, private, yours.

Backend 2 - OpenRouter, cost $0.00. Create a key at openrouter.ai, then swap the two lines as below and run again. Same messy text, now extracted by a 405B - notice whether the output actually differs on an easy task (usually: barely, which is itself a lesson).

Backend 3 - Nous Portal (if you have a key). Swap the two lines again: base_url https://inference-api.nousresearch.com/v1, your Portal key, and a Hermes 4 model id from the Portal's model list. The free eval tier's ~$0.10 of monthly credits covers this comfortably.

Cost math. Estimate your per-call tokens: our extraction prompt is roughly 800 tokens in (schema + messy text) and 200 out. Now price a 10,000-call/month workload on Hermes 4 70B at the listed rates: 8M input tokens × $0.13/1M = $1.04, plus 2M output × $0.40/1M = $0.80. About $1.84/month - and now scale the same math to YOUR real prompt sizes and volumes before trusting it.

Land the point: nothing about your code is provider-shaped. You are one config file away from routing per workload - which is exactly what Demo 2 decides.

★ The only two lines that change per backend# Backend 1 - local Ollama client = OpenAI(base_url="http://localhost:11434/v1", api_key="ollama") model = "hf.co/bartowski/NousResearch_Hermes-4-14B-GGUF:Q4_K_M" # Backend 2 - OpenRouter (free 405B) client = OpenAI(base_url="https://openrouter.ai/api/v1", api_key="sk-or-...") model = "nousresearch/hermes-3-llama-3.1-405b:free" # Backend 3 - Nous Portal (first-party) client = OpenAI(base_url="https://inference-api.nousresearch.com/v1", api_key="your-portal-key") model = "<pick a Hermes 4 id from the Portal model list>"
Prices dated mid-2026 The $0.13/$0.40 and $1.00/$3.00 figures are anchors as of mid-2026. Marketplace prices move monthly; re-check openrouter.ai listings and the Portal pricing page (in a browser - it is bot-gated) before quoting numbers to anyone with a budget.

Pick your production tier ★ 6 min · worksheet

No new code - a decision exercise against your own life. Three real workloads, classified with the Part 3 table. This worksheet is what you will actually reuse after the course.

Write down three REAL workloads you have built or want to build with your assistant. Examples from past cohorts: "summarize client interview notes", "triage inbound email", "draft LinkedIn posts", "extract line items from receipts".

For each, answer the lane-1 question first: does it touch sensitive data (client, personal, regulated), or will it run at high volume (thousands of calls)? Either yes → local. Write it down and move on - privacy vetoes everything else.

For the remainder: is your local model actually good enough for it? Test, do not guess - run one hard example on your 14B. If yes → local anyway (free beats cheap). If it visibly struggles → next step.

Occasional and experimental → free-tier API (OpenRouter :free, Portal eval credits). Recurring, team-facing, or uptime-critical → paid API, and run the Demo 1 cost math on its real volume before committing.

Sanity-check the result: most people land on 2 local + 1 hosted. If all three landed hosted, revisit whether you are underrating your 14B; if all three landed local, enjoy your electricity bill being the whole budget.

Real world

The three-lane consultant. An independent strategy consultant classified her stack exactly this way: interview-note summarization stayed local (client confidentiality, non-negotiable), a weekly research digest runs on the OpenRouter free 405B (occasional, zero cost), and a client-facing FAQ bot runs on a paid 70B endpoint (needs uptime her laptop cannot promise). Total monthly API bill: under $5.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · Moving your Session 5 extractor from local Ollama to the Nous Portal requires changing...

Local Ollama, the Portal, and OpenRouter all speak the OpenAI-compatible API. Same code, different base_url is the whole architecture of this session.

2 · You are shipping Hermes inside a commercial product and want the cleanest license story. Which bucket?

Hermes inherits its base model's license. 4.3 36B is Apache 2.0 and the 14B is Apache-2.0-family; the 70B/405B and Hermes 3 carry the Llama 3 license with Meta's conditions (700M MAU clause, naming). Open weights is not public domain.

3 · A workload touches confidential client data AND your 14B handles it fine. Where does it run?

Privacy is the first gate in the decision table: sensitive data stays local, full stop. Free API is still someone else's computer under someone else's ToS.

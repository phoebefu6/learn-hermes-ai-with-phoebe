Trained by the swarm: Psyche and DisTrO

In December 2025, Nous shipped Hermes 4.3-36B - the first production model post-trained entirely on a decentralized network of GPUs scattered across the open internet. This is the story of how that works, the controlled experiment that proved it, and how to read the claim with clear eyes.

Why this page exists

Every frontier model you have ever used was trained in a datacenter: one building, one owner, one bill measured in the hundreds of millions. Hermes 4.3 was not. It was post-trained by 24 independent nodes coordinating over the ordinary internet - and in a controlled head-to-head, that swarm-trained model beat its conventionally trained twin. Nous calls the framing "Local Intelligence Globally Trained". This deep dive unpacks the why, the how, and the honest caveats.

Part 1 · the thesis

Why decentralize training at all ~6 min read

Open weights solve half the ownership problem: anyone can RUN the model. But the ability to MAKE frontier models still belongs to whoever owns a datacenter full of tightly interconnected GPUs. Decentralized training attacks that last gate.

Self-studyThe gatekeeper problem3 min read▶

Run through what it takes to train (or seriously post-train) a frontier-class model today, and one fact dominates: the GPUs must talk to each other constantly, exchanging enormous gradient updates many times per second. That only works over specialized datacenter interconnects - which means the whole job must live in one building, and one building means one owner. Three consequences follow:

  • A single gatekeeper. Whoever owns the datacenter decides which models get made, with what values, released under what terms. Open weights give you the artifact; the means of production stay closed.
  • Stranded compute. The world is full of GPUs that are idle, scattered, or too small alone to matter - community rigs, university clusters, small providers. A datacenter-only paradigm makes all of that irrelevant.
  • A single point of pressure. One building, one company, one jurisdiction: easy to regulate, pressure, or switch off. A training run spread across independent nodes in many places has no single plug to pull.

The decentralization thesis in one line: if training can run over the open internet, then making models stops being a privilege of datacenter owners - the same move open weights made for running them.

Real world

The precedent. Folding@home and SETI@home showed decades ago that scattered volunteer machines can add up to world-class compute for problems that tolerate loose coordination. The open question was always whether LLM training - the most communication-hungry workload in computing - could ever be made to tolerate it.

Self-studyLocal Intelligence Globally Trained2 min read▶

Nous frames Hermes 4.3 with the slogan "Local Intelligence Globally Trained", and it is worth pausing on how neatly the two halves of the course thesis snap together:

  • Local intelligence - the part you already live: open weights, running on your machine, answering to your system prompt. Sessions 1-8.
  • Globally trained - the new part: the model was also MADE outside any single company's walls, by a network anyone with GPUs can in principle join.

Whether or not decentralized training becomes the norm, the direction of the argument matters: it extends the ownership story from inference (running models) to training (making them). Keep that frame in mind - and keep your skeptic's hat on until Part 4, where we stress-test it.

Part 2 · covers the 4.3 announcement's systems story

The machinery: DisTrO, Psyche, and a blockchain ~7 min read

Three named pieces make the swarm work: DisTrO (the algorithm that shrinks what nodes must say to each other), Psyche (the network that organizes the nodes), and Solana (the neutral referee keeping them honest). Plain words for each.

Self-studyDisTrO - training over slow wires3 min read▶

In normal distributed training, every GPU computes gradients - "here is how I would nudge every weight in the model" - and all GPUs merge their nudges before taking the next step. For a 36B-parameter model that is a gigantic message, exchanged constantly. Datacenter interconnects exist precisely to survive this firehose; your home fiber cannot.

DisTrO (Distributed Training Over-the-Internet) is Nous's family of optimizers built on one bet: the firehose is mostly redundant. By radically compressing what nodes exchange - transmitting only the informative core of each update rather than the full raw gradient - DisTrO cuts the required bandwidth so far that ordinary internet links become good enough to train over.

  • The mental model: instead of shipping the entire markup of everything you would change, ship a tight summary that captures what matters. Everyone stays in sync on much thinner wires.
  • The cost: compression and coordination are not free - there is overhead, and making convergence behave took years of iteration. Psyche also trained an earlier proof model, Consilience 40B, before 4.3 made it to production.
Why this is the load-bearing piece Everything else in this story is organization. DisTrO is the physics: without the compression breakthrough, "train over the open internet" is simply impossible, no matter how clever the network around it is.
Self-studyPsyche - the network, and the numbers2 min read▶

Psyche is the coordination layer that turns DisTrO from an algorithm into a working training network: it splits the job, hands shards to nodes, collects and merges their compressed updates, and keeps the run marching forward even though the workers are independent machines in different places. The Hermes 4.3 run, in numbers Nous published:

DimensionThe 4.3 run
Nodes24 independent nodes over the open internet
ThroughputAveraging 144k tokens/sec across the swarm
Post-training corpus~5M samples · ~60B tokens
Base modelByteDance Seed-OSS-36B · 512K context
LicenseApache 2.0 - the clean, permissive one

Keep the scale honest: 24 nodes is a coordinated network of serious GPU operators, not (yet) a million laptops folding proteins. The claim being tested is narrower and more falsifiable: can a real production model be post-trained this way at all, without giving up quality? Part 3 is the answer.

Self-studyWhat Solana is doing in the loop2 min read▶

A decentralized run has a trust problem a datacenter never faces: the workers do not belong to you. Who decides the authoritative order of updates? What stops a faulty - or malicious - node from corrupting the run? You need a referee that no single participant controls.

Psyche's answer is to use the Solana blockchain as its coordination and security layer: the shared, tamper-evident record of the run that all nodes can verify, exactly because consensus on an untrusted network is the one problem blockchains were actually built to solve.

  • The charitable read: a neutral referee for mutually untrusting parties is a legitimate engineering requirement here, and reusing battle-tested consensus beats inventing your own.
  • The skeptical read: "AI + crypto" is a phrase that has decorated a lot of vapor, and the association imposes a real credibility tax. Note honestly that the blockchain coordinates the training; the learning itself is DisTrO and GPUs.

Both reads fit the facts. Hold them together until Part 4.

Part 3 · the scientific highlight

The twin experiment ~5 min read

"Swarm training works" would be easy to claim and hard to check. So Nous did the thing you almost never see a vendor do: they trained the same model twice - once the normal way, once on Psyche - and published both, as a controlled comparison anyone can rerun.

Self-studyThe setup, and why it is good science3 min read▶

The design is exactly what you would demand from a skeptic's chair. Hold everything constant - same Seed-OSS-36B base, same ~60B-token post-training corpus - and vary only the training infrastructure:

  • Twin A, centralized: a conventional datacenter run on TorchTitan FSDP with the AdamW optimizer - the industry-standard recipe, as the control.
  • Twin B, decentralized: the Psyche network - 24 nodes, DisTrO compression, Solana coordination, over the open internet.

Then run both through the same eval suite. The result: the Psyche-trained twin won. Not "came close", not "acceptable quality loss" - the swarm-trained model scored better, and it is the one that shipped as Hermes 4.3. The production model's own numbers stand on their own: it matches or exceeds Hermes 4 70B at roughly half the parameters, with AIME'24 71.9, MATH-500 93.8, MMLU 87.7, BBH 86.4, and a 74.6 non-reasoning RefusalBench score (state of the art among popular models - deep dive D5 teaches you to read that one critically).

Real world

The part nobody else does. Labs bury failed baselines. Nous released the losing twin - NousResearch/Hermes-4.3-36B-centralized on HuggingFace - so anyone can download both models and check the comparison themselves. Publishing your control group is genuinely unique in the industry, and it is what lifts this from a marketing claim to an experiment.

Self-studyWhat the result does and does not prove2 min read▶

Sharpen the conclusion before Part 4 stress-tests it:

  • Proven: decentralized post-training over the open internet can produce a production-quality frontier finetune with no quality penalty - in this controlled instance, an advantage.
  • Not proven: that this holds at every scale, for pretraining from scratch, or across many repeated runs. One excellent experiment is one data point, however well designed.
  • Worth savoring anyway: the null hypothesis for years was "internet-scale links are far too slow for serious training, full stop". That hypothesis is now dead, killed in public, with both bodies of evidence downloadable.
Part 4 · keep your head

Reading it critically ~4 min read

The course rule for vendor stories applies to stories we like, too. Here is the honest scorecard - enthusiasm and skepticism on the same page.

Self-studyThe caveats card3 min read▶
CaveatThe honest version
One proof point4.3 is the first production model trained this way (Consilience 40B was the earlier proof run). Extraordinary claims need replication; watch whether the next Psyche runs repeat the result.
Coordination overheadCompression, consensus, and stragglers all cost something. A swarm is not automatically cheaper or faster than a datacenter - the claim is that it is now POSSIBLE and competitive, not strictly superior.
The crypto couplingSolana consensus is an engineering answer to a real trust problem, but it welds the training story to a blockchain ecosystem, which raises eyebrows and adds a dependency that is not strictly about machine learning.
Scale unknown24 coordinated nodes post-training a 36B model is not yet "the world's idle GPUs pretraining a trillion-parameter model". The extrapolation is plausible, not demonstrated.

And the other column, held simultaneously: the twin experiment is real, controlled, and published including the control group; the evals favored the swarm; and the ownership argument - no single datacenter gatekeeper deciding which models exist - is the same argument that made open weights matter. Both columns can be true. That is what a fair reading looks like.

A note on sources The Psyche website is a JavaScript-only app that resists citation; treat the Hermes 4.3 announcement post as the citable source of record for everything on this page, and the HuggingFace artifact as the checkable evidence.

Check yourself

Three questions before you go 🎯 ◐ 90 seconds

1 · What is Psyche?

Psyche is the network layer: it organized the 24 nodes (averaging 144k tokens/sec) that post-trained Hermes 4.3 entirely outside any single datacenter, with Solana as the neutral coordination and security referee.

2 · What did the twin experiment show?

That is what makes it science rather than marketing: everything held constant except the infrastructure, the swarm won, and the losing baseline (Hermes-4.3-36B-centralized) was published for anyone to verify.

3 · What problem does DisTrO solve?

DisTrO is the physics of the whole story: distributed training over slow internet links, made possible by shrinking the communication, not the model. Without it, Psyche is just a nice org chart.
